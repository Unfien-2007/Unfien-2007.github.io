My journey through IT technically started when I realized that for someone like me who has a chronic illness, finding a job to earn a living would be a nightmare if I had to rely on physical labor.

That was 16-year-old me looking at the life I was currently living, a family in a struggling financial situation. If either of my parents ever lost their job, the continuation of my education would be an uncertainty that gave me a sky high amount of stress, especially since I felt completely helpless to do anything about it. 

At 16, I learned to not want things, to just be happy with what I had, and to embrace the philosophy: "hope for the best, prepare for the worst." I searched and searched for how to get a job without doing physical labor, but as the school year passed by, the "easy" path I was looking for never appeared. I realized that I would not get a job free of hard labor without being educated. 

My idea of a job at that age was sitting in an office, just typing at a computer. So, the IT path, which I only researched when I was picking my college course  became open to my eyes. That summer after graduation, I dreamed. I did try to learn, but I was just 17, freshly finished with SHS, and looking for a path that no one I personally knew had taken. My parents did not get a college education... 

...I was completely on my own if I actually wanted to learn.
